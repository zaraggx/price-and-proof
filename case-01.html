CASE 01 / AIRLINES

Why does your flight cost more than mine?

Imagine two passengers sitting next to each other. One paid £84. The other paid £219. Neither got a better seat.

THE QUESTION

What happens to the price as the flight gets closer?

Illustrative booking data14 days → departure
14 days£84
10 days£101
7 days£123
3 days£174
1 day£219

This is illustrative data for the prototype, not a claim about a particular airline.

YOUR FIRST THEORY

Maybe demand changes.

People booking one day before a flight may have fewer alternatives. A business traveller who has to be somewhere tomorrow can be less sensitive to price than someone planning a holiday months ahead.

THE ECONOMIC IDEA

Price elasticity of demand

If a small change in price causes a large change in quantity demanded, demand is relatively elastic. If quantity changes little, it is relatively inelastic.

CURRENT VERDICT

There isn't one magic “fair price”.

An airline can face different groups of customers with different willingness to pay. Pricing closer to departure can reflect changing demand, remaining capacity and the firm's attempt to maximise revenue.

BUT ARE WE SURE?

This isn't proof of price discrimination.

Our five observations don't tell us why the prices changed. Competitor prices, remaining seats, fuel costs, booking class and many other factors could matter. A better investigation would collect a larger dataset while controlling for these variables.
